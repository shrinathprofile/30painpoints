## The complaint (evidence)
- **Subreddit:** r/remotework
- **Thread / quote:** "Is there anyone else who forgets what was decided in the meetings?" — the thread's own answer is yes, repeatedly; sibling asks in the same space beg for free summarizers, and the tools people name (Otter, Fireflies) get called out as paywalled.
- **Why I believe it's recurring:** the pain is structural, not personal — meetings produce no artifact anyone owns, memory of "who said they'd do what" decays within the hour, and every paid notetaker gates the one feature the complaint actually needs (decisions + owners, not a wall of summary prose). The free gap between "full meeting AI suite" and "nothing" is exactly one transcript file wide.
- **Source URL:** https://www.reddit.com/r/remotework/comments/1qr195n/is_there_anyone_else_who_forgets_what_was_decided/ (evidence pre-verified 2026-10-24 in `docs/02-THE-30-IDEAS.md`; per the D04/D07/D09 precedent, Reddit's bot wall may block a re-fetch — the URL and vote counts are quoted from the verified idea card, never re-invented.)

## Why this should exist (ponytail rung 1)
The meeting already contains its own decisions — spoken, then lost; the artifact that survives is a transcript nobody rereads, and turning that transcript into a decisions/actions/owners list is pure extraction, which needs no meeting bot, no audio, no diarization, and no account — the paywalled suites sell the recording, but the complaint only asks for the mining.

## The people hurting
Remote team members who leave a call with a vague "someone's handling that, right?" feeling — several times a week — and today reread (or worse, don't) a 40-minute transcript to reconstruct three decisions; the ones with budgets pay Otter/Fireflies for it.

## Scope
- **30-minute floor (fallback):** stdlib-only CLI: read a transcript file, extract action-verb sentences by curated cue lists (decision / action / open-question), print them as Markdown with speaker + line citation. No LLM, no server. Done.
- **Today's target:** the floor, plus the two things that make it a tool instead of a grep: (1) **owner resolution** — `Name:`-labeled turns (plus `[HH:MM:SS]` timestamps and WebVTT/SRT exports incl. `<v Name>` voice tags), `NAME will VERB` third-party commits, `I'll`→speaker, `we'll`→team (flagged, never faked), due dates (`by Friday`, `EOD`, `next week`); a **reversal flag** ("actually… let's go with B" supersedes the prior decision — struck through, pointed at, never silently dropped); (2) **an optional `--llm` pass** (OpenAI-compatible endpoint via env, **LLM injected as a world callable**, D08 idiom) whose EVERY item must carry a verbatim quote **verified by substring against the transcript** — unverifiable claims are dropped and counted, never shown; LLM requested but undeliverable = exit 2 named, heuristic report still printed, never a silent fallback. Plus a FastAPI face (`server.py`, one POST endpoint + one inline-HTML drop page, native drag-drop, 2 MB upload guard) because "drop a transcript file" is a browser action and the complaint's audience isn't terminal-first.
- **Deliberately NOT building:** no meeting-record bots, no live audio, no speaker diarization (unlabeled transcripts say "no speaker labels — owners unknown" honestly), no calendar/Slack integration, no storage or accounts (file in, Markdown out, nothing kept), no "conversation resolution" (an "agreed?" followed by "yes" two turns later stays a question + a noise line — named ceiling), no embeddings/RAG over past meetings (one transcript, one report).
- **Guarding against (from MISTAKES.md/learnings, today's seams):** the transcript is an **untrusted file at a trust boundary** — read with `utf-8-sig` (BOM, D04), refuse undecodable bytes loudly (exit 2, never a guess), cap size; **LLM output is untrusted text at a second boundary** — quotes verified against the transcript before display, control chars rendered as Unicode pictures not raw bytes (D08's one-sanitize rule), output length capped; **assert the unit at every seam** (D08) — line numbers are 1-based human line numbers in citations, tested as such; **never inherit a provider's ordering** (D02) — items sorted chronologically by transcript position, LLM pass included; checks adjudicated by execution with fixtures **hand-verified twice** (D05), and the wrong-assertion count sits at three (D09) — expected values re-derived from the fixture by hand, not from the tool's own output.

## Stack of the day

## Definition of done
A stranger with any `.txt`/`.vtt` meeting transcript runs `python3 decision_miner.py transcript.txt` and gets, in under 5 seconds, a Markdown report of decisions, actions with owners and due dates, and open questions — every line citing speaker + timestamp/line it came from — exit 0; a transcript with nothing decidable says exactly that and exits 1; an unreadable file or an undeliverable `--llm` says why and exits 2 — verified by a self-check run (fixtures hand-verified twice) plus a browser drop of a transcript onto the served page producing the same report.
